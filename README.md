# Party Kart

Braga's real streets, to drive round in a kart with your friends, and two tracks to race them on:
the Volta dos Amigos, past the friends' houses, and the Volta de Lamaçães. In the browser, on a
keyboard or a phone, with power-ups and bots in a race. A throwaway game: no server, no accounts, nothing to maintain but the map it's played
on, which lives with its photos in braga-map. The drivers are the
friends, and all faces are cartoons.

## Playing

The game speaks Portuguese, Portugal's.

The game opens with you driving in Braga, alone: on the granite plinth at the start on the Rua do
Fujacal, or where you were when you last played. Drive anywhere in the Volta dos Amigos' area: down
every street, over the pavements, across the squares, along the footways and the cycle lanes, up
and down the city's steps a bump at a time, as far as the road-works barriers where the streets
and paths leave it. The minimap round you shows the streets, the race's route in gold and the area's
edge in red, the way you face at the top, and the street's name as you drive onto it. At every
corner a blue-and-white plate names each street that begins there, on the corner building's wall, or
on a post where no building stands at the corner; and every bus stop stands on its pavement, a
shelter where the map has one.

**M** opens the full map over the game, which goes on under it while your kart waits (or you stand):
the whole open world and the city round it, north up, the streets at their widths and their names
as you zoom in, the race's route in gold, the three arenas in orange, the area's edge in red, and you
and the others on it as on the minimap. The wheel zooms, a drag moves it, **⌖ Onde estou** brings
it back round you, and M or Esc closes it. Flying or on foot it's the same; in a race it opens on the
whole track; in a round, on its arena, with only you and your team-mates on it, never a foe. On a
touch screen a tap on the minimap opens it, two fingers zoom, and **✕** closes it.

**The GPS**: round the city, a click on the full map (a tap) puts a waypoint there, and a click on
it takes it away. The route to it from where you are goes along the real streets by the rules of the
road: the one-way streets the right way, no turn the map bans, no turning back but at a dead end, the
avenues before the side streets; and only where no street reaches the waypoint inside the area (the
Picoto's top, whose streets come in from outside it), up the paths and the steps, as a kart can. It's
in blue on the minimap and the full map, as arrows on the street for the next 100 m, and at the top of
the screen: the next turn, how far it is and the street it turns onto ("Vira à direita na Rua …"),
and how far there is to go. Leave the route and it's worked out again from where you are; on foot
it's a walker's, any way along the streets and the paths, up and down the steps. Once you're there it
says so and clears. It's yours alone, and not in a race or a round, whose own routes rule.

**Ir para aqui**: round the city, a right click on the full map (a long press on a touch screen)
opens a menu with **🚀 Ir para aqui**, which puts your kart on the street nearest that place, on the
ground (not a bridge over it), facing along it, and closes the map; on foot it puts you there, your
kart left where it was; flying, the camera, as high over the ground as it was. Outside the area the
menu says **Fora da zona**. The others see you go from one place to the other, with no trail across
the city. Never in a race or a round.

**Esc**, or **☰ Menu** at the top, opens the menu over the world, which goes on behind it: your name
and driver, the graphics, the help, and

1. **Convidar amigos**: a room of your own, and its link to send your friends (**Copiar o convite**,
   or **Partilhar o convite** on a phone). Whoever opens it drives into your world, on the street
   next to you. Each of you sees the others where they are, their names over them, and the feed says
   who came and went. **Entrar** joins a friend's room by its code.
2. **🏁 Corrida**: the host chooses the track, the mode, the laps or minutes, the items, catch-up and the bots,
   and **Começar a corrida** puts everyone in the room on the grid, the empty places filled with
   bots. When it's over the results show over the world, and everyone is back where they were,
   driving.
3. **🎯 Tiroteio**: a deathmatch on foot after Counter-Strike 2's, in one of three small arenas in
   Fujacal and the Conselheiro Lobato, about 80 m across each, everyone for themselves or in two teams: **Topo do Fujacal**, the Rua do Fujacal
   by the race's start round a block of flats alone in its street; **Doutor José Vilaça**, the flats'
   courtyard off the Rua Monsenhor Airosa; and **Santa Justa**, the street by the river and the
   Ecovia. Most of their buildings open on the ground floor, a room you walk into through its door
   and shoot out of through its windows; and in Topo do Fujacal and Doutor José Vilaça a block of
   flats goes up by its stairs, floor by floor, to its roof. The host chooses the arena, everyone for
   themselves or two teams (**Equipa Azul** and **Equipa Laranja**, shared out evenly, the bots
   too), how long (3, 5 or 10 minutes), how many bots fill the empty places and how good they are
   (**Fácil**, **Médio** or **Difícil**), and starts it: everyone in the room is put on foot at a
   spawn there, their kart waiting where it was, with the buy menu open till the start. The money is
   unlimited, as in Counter-Strike's deathmatch: twenty guns (pistols, sub-machine guns, rifles,
   sniper's rifles, shotguns and a machine gun), the knife, four grenades (HE, flash, smoke and
   molotov), and a vest and helmet; what you buy stays with you every time you come back, and you can
   change it in the first 15 s of a life (after that, or while you're down, it's for the next). Alone
   with bots, it's a round worth playing too. Whoever (or whichever team) has the most kills when the
   time runs out wins; then the results, with each player's share of kills to the head, and everyone
   is back in their kart. Put down, you fall, the camera turns to whoever did it, and after three
   seconds you come back where none of your foes can see you, unhurt for a moment. Health doesn't
   come back; armour takes part of each hit to the body (to the head too, with a helmet).
   A railing round the arena, and road-works barriers across the streets and paths that leave it,
   keep the round in it; they stand only while a round is on. The bots are the drivers nobody picked,
   on foot as a player is: each chooses a kit as it comes back, goes looking round the arena, in and
   out of the buildings and up the stairs, turns to a shot or footsteps it hears, takes cover when
   it's hurt, and sees and shoots only what a player there could. On Fácil they're slow to react and
   miss a lot; on Difícil they're quick and true. Shot, you see where from: a red arc round the
   crosshair points at whoever fired, and turns as you turn. Anyone else hit is plain to see from
   anywhere: a tracer from the shooter's gun to them, a red spray blown out the far side, and their
   figure jerked away from the shot.
4. **↺ Reposicionar**: back on the street nearest.
5. **🎥 Voar pela cidade**: leave your kart where it is and fly the camera round Braga, down a
   street, through the buildings, over the rooftops and the hills as far as the horizon, from a metre
   over the ground to 500 m; the higher, the faster. Your kart waits where you left it, and the
   others see it parked. **🏎️ Voltar ao kart** (or V) puts it on the street under the camera, to drive
   on from there. A race the host starts ends the flight, and you're back in your kart where it
   waited when it's over.
6. **🚶 A pé**: get out of your kart and walk Braga in first person, at a person's height: along the
   pavements, up kerbs and flights of steps, through passages, stopped by every wall, fence, hedge,
   gate, building, tree and kart. Your kart waits where it stopped, empty, and the others see you
   standing beside it as the driver you picked, walking or running, and hear your footsteps. You're
   armed, as in a round: what you last bought in your hands, and the buy menu (B) yours whenever you
   want it, the money unlimited. Your shots leave their marks and your grenades go off, seen and heard
   by the others on foot, but round the city nobody's hurt: the bullets go through people.
   **🏎️ Voltar ao kart** (or F) puts you back in it where it waited. A race the host starts puts you
   back in it first.
7. **⚙️ Definições**: the settings, kept in your browser. *Gráficos*: the quality, stepping down by
   itself when the game's slow (or not), the resolution, the field of view on foot and driving, and
   the camera's shake. *Som*: all of it, the engines, the effects and guns, the city round you, and the
   jingles, each its own level. *Rato e mira*: the mouse's sensitivity, through a scope too, the
   vertical axis upside down, a finger's on a touch screen; and the crosshair (its colour, length,
   thickness, gap, opacity, a dot, its outline, and whether it opens as your shots would spread),
   with a preview. *Ecrã*: the keys' line at the bottom, the minimap, the frames a second. *Controlos*:
   every control's keys, for driving, flying, on foot and in a round, each its own: ✕ takes a key off,
   + binds another by pressing it (any key, or a mouse button or the wheel); a key another control of
   the same mode had is taken from it. Esc is always the menu.

If the host leaves, everyone else drives on, alone.

**Race**: 1 to 3 laps (a lap is about four and a half minutes), a starting grid, positions, power-ups, catch-up. **Time attack**: everyone
drives at once, through each other; your best lap before the clock runs out counts, against
the ghost of your best. Round the city there are no items.

The deathmatch's guns are Counter-Strike 2's (`src/game/arsenal.ts`), each with its damage (a hit
on the head counts four times, on the legs three quarters), how much of it gets through armour, how
it falls off with distance, its rate of fire, magazine and reserve, reload and draw times, and how
fast you run with it out. A gun is true standing still or walking, less so running, crouched truer
still, and wild in the air; let go of the keys (or press the other way, to counter-strafe) and you
stop at once. You move as in Counter-Strike: a jump is a press (holding Space doesn't hop again; bind
it to the wheel, as many do, in ⚙️ Definições), a hop pressed as you land keeps your speed, and in the
air strafing as you turn the mouse the same way gains it: a bunny hop. Each automatic's spray climbs and sways along its own pattern, the same every time, so
you learn to pull against it; the first shot goes where you aim. The AWP and the SSG 08 have two
zooms, and fired without them (a no-scope) the AWP's shot goes anywhere near the crosshair; a moment
after the scope comes up it's dead on (a quick-scope); after each shot the bolt takes the scope down,
and it comes back up by itself. The SG 553 and the AUG have a scope with one zoom. The knife cuts
(click) or stabs (right button), and from behind it's worth much more. Grenades go where you look:
overarm with the click, lobbed with the right button, between with both; they bounce off walls and
the ground. The HE grenade blasts up to 98 close to it; the flash blinds whoever looks at it; the
smoke makes a cloud nobody sees through for 18 s (the bots neither); the molotov sets the ground
alight for 7 s, and a smoke puts it out. Falls of more than a storey hurt.

**The track, the Volta dos Amigos**: 10.1 km of Braga as it is, through five friends' neighbourhoods
south and east of the centre, the way the traffic goes. Every street, building, park and tree round
it comes from OpenStreetMap, and the ground from DGT's 2025 LiDAR: the hills, 68 m from the lowest
point to the highest and as steep as 19.5%, the streets that tip sideways, the cuttings and banks
beside them. From the line on the Rua do Fujacal, west as its traffic goes, round by the Avenida
Imaculada Conceição, down the Avenida da Liberdade and over the Rio Este at São João da Ponte; up
past the Largo da Devesa to the slopes of the Picoto; up the Avenida 31 de Janeiro and the Avenida
João XXI and back down the Rua Bernardo Sequeira, then Lamaçães's avenues and roundabouts, past
Makro and the Tourigalo; back west round the Picoto by the Picoto Adventure Park, and down
the Avenida Doutor Viriato Amaral Nunes past the Estádio 1.º de Maio and the Altice Forum; west by
Ponte Pedrinha and down a straight street to the Rio Este, where the road works have left a ramp of
planks on a pile of rubble: the race jumps the river on it into a dead end, the one way in, and comes
out by its mouth to go home along the Rua Conselheiro Lobato. The ramp is the one thing on the
track that isn't in the real Braga, and it's only there in a race.

**The Volta de Lamaçães**: 9.2 km of the streets east of the Avenida da Liberdade, inside the same
area, with no jump. From the line on the Avenida João XXI, down the Rua Bernardo Sequeira to the
Largo de Santa Tecla, the Rua de Baixo and the Avenida Doutor Porfírio da Silva; the side streets
under the Picoto to the Via da Falperra and the Rua Simões de Almeida; north through the streets of
Lamaçães to the Avenida Robert Smith, up the Rua Irmãos Sousa and the Rua Afonso Palmeira to the
Avenida Doutor António Alves Palha; and back west by Santa Tecla, the Avenida da Liberdade and the
Rua André Soares. A third of it is on the Volta dos Amigos' streets, some of them the other way, and
the rest is narrower and twistier: a lap takes about as long, at a lower speed. The friends' eggs
stay on their houses, by the Volta dos Amigos, and go off only in its race.

Every street round it is drawn as it is, at its real width: its lanes painted white, its zebra
crossings, traffic lights and lamps, calçada pavements behind granite kerbs, central reservations
and islands where it has them, each roundabout's ring and island as they really are, its island's
grass and flower beds and the give-way lines and triangles at its entries, granite setts where it's
cobbled, its bridges and tunnels, and
road-works barriers where it leaves the Volta dos Amigos' area. So is every footway, path and
cycleway, the cycle lanes in red, and every flight of steps, each step at its height, with its
handrails where it has them. The race takes its streets as they
are, at their real widths: a narrow one is as narrow in a race, its kerbs are the race's walls, and a
row of the race's barriers closes every side street it passes. Only while it's on do its start line,
gates, boost pads and barriers stand in them; and where the jump lands, the corner of a walled lot is
the race's, its walls down and road works' boards round it. Bom Jesus and the
Sameiro stand on the hills to the east, drawn twice their size so you can find them; on top of the
Picoto its white steel cross, and the miradouro's boardwalk round it. São João da Ponte's chapel
stands in its park by the Avenida da Liberdade, its bell gable over its door and its porch before
it, and the avenue crosses the Este there on a granite arch. The Estádio 1.º de Maio is its bowl of
terraces as they really rise, walled outside, with its four floodlights, and the Altice Forum has
its α. By the Avenida Doutor António Alves Palha's roundabout stands the Tourigalo, the
black bull with a rooster's head, on its lawn: five times its real size, and exaggerated in every
part, on purpose. São João Baptista stands in São João da Ponte's roundabout, in white stone on his

The first track, the cartoon Braga Grand Prix (the Avenida, the Sé, the switchbacks of Bom Jesus
and the jump), is kept in the code for reference, but the game doesn't race it any more.

The drivers are the five friends, each in a car that's a joke about them: Pedro's tech kart (a laptop
on the nose, a Wi-Fi antenna, neon underneath), Joel's dentist's chair, Joel F.'s Mercadona trolley,
Capelas's car for sale for parts (rusty, roofless, its bumper hanging off), and Vale's kart with a

**Graphics** (*Gráficos*): *Alta*, *Média* or *Baixa*, in the menu, and the rest in **⚙️ Definições**.
Phones start on Medium. If the game runs slowly, it steps down a level by itself, as far as Low (the

The keys in the tables are the defaults: every one can be changed in **⚙️ Definições › Controlos**,
and the keys' line at the bottom and the help say them as they're bound.

**Competitivo**, the menu's 🎯 Tiroteio with *Modo: Competitivo (bomba)*: Counter-Strike 2's
competitive mode in the same three arenas, two teams of up to four, the empty places bots. One team
starts as the Terrorists (orange), the other as the Counter-Terrorists (blue), each at its own end of
the arena; the two bomb sites, **A** and **B**, are nearer the Counter-Terrorists' (their letters show
over them, through the walls, and their edges are painted on the ground). Nobody comes back in a
round: put down, you watch a team-mate (a click for the next). A round starts with 15 s frozen at your
spawn, buying; the buy menu sells there for 20 s more, at Counter-Strike's prices, each side its own
guns (the AK-47, the Galil and the SG 553 the Terrorists', the M4s, the FAMAS and the AUG the
Counter-Terrorists', a defuse kit theirs too). Then 1:55. One Terrorist carries the bomb: hold **E**
in a site for 3.2 s to plant it (standing still), and it goes off 40 s later, its beeps quicker as
the time runs out; a Counter-Terrorist holds **E** by it for 10 s (5 with a kit) to defuse it. The
Terrorists win the round by putting the Counter-Terrorists down, or by the bomb going off; the
Counter-Terrorists by putting the Terrorists down before a plant, by defusing it, or by the time
running out. The money is Counter-Strike's: $800 to start each half, $3250 for a round won ($3500
by the bomb or a defuse), $1400 for one lost and $500 more for each lost in a row after it (to
$3400), $800 to each Terrorist for a round lost after a plant, $300 for planting or defusing, a kill
by the gun ($300 a rifle's, $600 a sub-machine gun's, $900 a pump shotgun's, $1500 the knife's, $100
the AWP's), $16000 at most. What you buy is yours while you live, the guns full again each round;
put down, you drop your gun where you fell (walk over it with your slot empty to pick it up, or **E**
by it to swap), and the bomb if you carried it. **G** drops the gun in your hand, or the bomb. After
half the rounds the sides swap and the money's back to $800; the first to win more than half the
rounds wins: *Curta*, the first to 9 of 16, or *Completa*, the first to 13 of 24 (12 all is a draw).
Tab shows each player's kills and deaths, your team-mates' money, and who's down.

## How it works

- Everyone drives their own car in their own browser, so there's no lag on your own driving.
  Whoever creates the room is the host: their browser relays where everyone is (15 times a second,
  round the city as in a race), and in a race drives the bots, relays what they throw, keeps the
  order and the clock. In a round its browser runs the bots on foot, and decides their shots.
- What's thrown is deterministic: a bola is where it started, how fast and when, so every
  browser draws it in the same place without sending positions. A hit is decided by the one
  who gets hit, in their own browser, and told to the others: what you see is what happened.
- A shot in the deathmatch is the other way round: a bullet is instant, so it's decided by the
  shooter's browser, against the others as it draws them, and the victim's browser takes the damage
  (and its armour takes its share). What hit on your screen hit. A grenade is like a bola: its throw
  is its whole flight, worked out the same in every browser, and each browser works out what its
  blast, flash or fire does to its own player. The host keeps the kills and deaths.
- Players connect over WebRTC, through [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com/)'s free public server, which
  only introduces them (and relays when a router is strict).

## Code

- `src/track/`: the road built from a layout, and the first track (`layout.ts`, for reference).
  The Volta dos Amigos itself comes from the map of Braga, below.
- `src/car/`: the handling (drifts, mini-turbos, jumps), and an autopilot.
- `src/game/`: a race: the arena (clock, cars, items in flight, order), a racer (laps, boxes,
  items, hits, bumps, slipstreams, catch-up), the items' rules, and the bots; a car driving round
  the city (`roamer.ts`); the flying camera (`flyer.ts`); you on foot (`walker.ts`); the
  deathmatch: its guns (`arsenal.ts`), a fighter, health, armour, shots and scores (`fight.ts`),
  grenades (`nades.ts`), an arena's field and spawns (`field.ts`); and its bots (`footbot.ts`); the
  competitive mode's rules, money, sites and spawns, and the host's match (`comp.ts`), and its bots'
  part in it (`matchbots.ts`).
- `src/world/`: the world as the karts' ground: the land, every street and path at its level, the
  steps, and the walls (`ground.ts`); the arenas' buildings' insides (`interiors.ts`, laid out by
  `storeys.ts` as their facades are); line of sight through it (`sight.ts`); and where someone on
  foot can walk, for the bots (`paths.ts`).
- `src/render/`: the look (sky, sun, shadows, quality); the city round the track (`city.ts`,
  from the map), every street and path in its area (`streets.ts`), what the race adds to them (`road.ts`),
  the bridges' pillars and the tunnels' mouths (`levels.ts`), the easter eggs (`eggs.ts`); trees, karts and
  drivers, items, particles, skid marks; the deathmatch's weapons in your hands, bullet marks and
  edge (`arms.ts`), the others as fighters (`soldier.ts`), the grenades (`nades.ts`), and a match's
  sites, bomb and guns on the ground (`comp.ts`); and the first track's world (`world.ts`,
  `landmarks.ts`, `braga.ts`), for reference.
- The map of Braga is its own repo, [braga-map](https://github.com/pedroHenriques15/braga-map),
  shared with the games to come: the city in sections, and the tracks planned on its streets. The
  game installs it as a package.
- `src/session.ts`: rooms. `src/hud.ts`: what's on the screen while you drive, in a race and round
  the city, and the full map (its sums in `src/world/mapview.ts`). `src/main.ts`: the menu, the race's screens, and the loop. `src/binds.ts`: every
  control and its default keys; `src/prefs.ts`: the settings, kept in the browser;
  `src/settingsScreen.ts`: ⚙️ Definições.
- `src/audio.ts`: every sound, through one mix. The karts' engines are a race car's V8 through six
  gears, synthesized, as is everything else but the city round the track: recordings of traffic,
  a town, birds, woods, a river, bells, a bus and a crowd (CC0, from Freesound, listed in
  braga-map's `docs/SOURCES.md`), in `src/sounds/`, heard by what's near your kart wherever it is
  (`src/ambience.ts`).
- `src/fight/`: the deathmatch's CC0 models (the guns, the knife, the grenades, the fighter, the
  cover's props) and recordings (shots, reloads, grenades, impacts, footsteps), each with its source
  in `src/fight/ASSETS.md`.

How it fits together, the network messages included, is in [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md).
How to change the track, add an item or a driver, and what to watch out for is in

The tests run the TypeScript straight in Node, which needs Node 22.18 or later.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:5173; ?autodrive lets the autopilot drive a race, for testing
npm test         # the track; the world's ground and free roam; flying; on foot; the deathmatch; the first track's jump; items, catch-up, slipstreams; eight bots
npm run check    # the type check (Node runs the tests without one)
npm run build
npm run deploy   # publish to GitHub Pages (the gh-pages branch)
```
